# Hero reference research

9 October 2026. Perplexity research using primary sources. Accessible page content was researched; live animation was not independently verified.

**Recommendation: make the rights decision—not an abstract AI effect—the hero’s moving object.** Each of the three directions below follows the same readable sequence: **source clause → proposed purpose and destination → decision → supporting evidence**. The motion changes what a visitor understands at each step; the final state remains visible.

### What the official homepages establish

These are observations from the official pages’ accessible content, **not a frame-by-frame browser inspection**. A depicted workflow, repeated text, or video timecode does not by itself verify playback, hover behavior, or scroll animation.

### Three hero directions within **Evidence in Motion**

All three use **Manrope**, cream paper surfaces, ink typography and restrained coral/lilac/sage status accents. The clause and request shown in a prototype should be **clearly labelled illustrative** until approved demonstration material exists.

| Hero concept | Composition: clause → purpose/destination → decision/evidence | Purposeful interaction and motion | Why it is distinct |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Annotated Folio** | Left: a large, tactile paper folio with a legible source-clause excerpt and citation marker. Right: a compact request card specifying **intended purpose** and **destination**. A decision slip emerges beside the relevant clause, with an evidence drawer showing the clause location and the request attributes considered. | Selecting a highlighted clause opens its margin note; changing the request’s destination visibly re-evaluates the *illustrative* case. Animate only the physical acts of locating the passage, aligning the request and revealing the decision. Never animate the clause away before it can be read. | Editorial and counsel-first: visitors start with the authority, not the software chrome. Immuta’s represented request-to-outcome sequence is the useful precedent. [web:16] |
| **2. The Waypoint Bench** | A spacious, three-station workbench: **Source**, **Proposed use**, **Decision**. A single folio card occupies the first station; purpose and destination are explicit chips in the second; the third contains a prominent status and an expandable evidence ledger. | Visitor-controlled **Next / Previous** moves the same case between stations. At each transition, retain a small breadcrumb of the prior inputs; selecting a chip updates the decision explanation without pretending to grant legal clearance. Optional desktop scroll-scrub can advance the stations, but buttons remain the primary, reversible controls. | Process-first and retrieval-engineer-friendly: the relationship between input fields and output is visible at a glance, without circuitry or decorative connecting lines. |
| **3. The Decision Docket** | Split composition: a narrow incoming-use request on one side; on the other, a larger docket where the cited clause, intended purpose, destination, provisional decision and evidence entries stack as paper tabs. The final tab is an explicit **needs review** state when evidence is insufficient. | Clicking **Inspect basis** unfolds the exact excerpt and the fields used; switching between two *labelled sample requests* changes only affected docket entries. A brief paper-tab shift signals what changed—no looping “processing” theater. | Decision-first and executive-readable: governance counsel sees the disposition immediately, then can inspect its basis; engineers can see which inputs drove it. |

**Motion and evidence guardrails.** If the Waypoint Bench uses GSAP, use `ScrollTrigger`’s `scrub` to link progress to scroll and `pin` only for a short desktop section; animate children rather than the pinned element. `gsap.matchMedia()` can remove that treatment at smaller widths or under `prefers-reduced-motion`, leaving the same case navigable as static, ordered steps. [web:2][web:71] Provide a visible motion-off option for nonessential interaction-triggered movement. If any hero sequence starts automatically and moves for more than five seconds alongside other content, provide a persistent pause/stop/hide mechanism; auto-updating content has no five-second exemption. Prefer **no autoplay** here: the visitor should control the pace of reading a clause and checking its evidence. [web:4][web:62]
